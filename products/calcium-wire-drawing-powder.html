Process Engineering

Shop-Floor Operational Guidance

Practical machine-setup recommendations from over three decades of on-site wire mill technical troubleshooting.

precision_manufacturing

1. Die Box Fluidity & Anti-Tunneling Control

Poor powder circulation in the entry die box causes 'tunneling' or rat-holing, where the moving wire draws an empty channel through the powder, causing immediate lubricant starvation and die galling. Our calcium powders are produced with controlled granular sizing to ensure continuous gravity replenishment directly into the die reduction cone without clumping.

precision_manufacturing

2. Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer Formation

Inside the tungsten carbide die, wire drawing generates substantial contact heat. At temperatures between 140°C and 160°C, our calcium soap undergoes a plastic phase transition, melting into a tenacious, high-pressure hydrodynamic boundary film that cushions the wire surface during heavy 20% to 30% area reductions per die.

precision_manufacturing

3. Mechanically Descaled vs Pickled Wire Rods

Mechanically descaled (MD) rods carry micro-fine residual iron scale that accelerates tungsten carbide die wear. For MD rods, select Grade 85 Black or D-45 to bind with scale particulates. For chemically pickled rods treated with lime or borax carriers, Grade PKL or P-75 ensures optimum carrier adhesion and an exceptionally bright finish.

precision_manufacturing

4. Die Geometry & Internal Block Cooling

For mild steel drafting using calcium lubricants, maintain an included die approach angle of 12° to 16° with a bearing length of 30% to 40% of wire diameter. Ensure continuous die jacket cooling with clean water flow (5 to 8 L/min per die box) to prevent overheating and soap burning inside the die housing.

Industrial Knowledge Base

Frequently Asked Questions

help_outline Why is calcium stearate powder preferred for mild steel (MS) wire drawing?

Calcium stearate is water-insoluble and maintains a plastic melting range of 140°C to 160°C. It forms a dense, durable hydrodynamic boundary film that withstands extreme contact pressures during rod breakdown and continuous drafting, preventing metal-to-metal galling and die wear.

help_outline What is the difference between D-45, P-75, PKL, and 85 Black grades?

D-45 is our versatile all-rounder for rod breakdown and continuous passes from 5.0mm down to 2.0mm. P-75 features an enhanced stearate carrier to extend tungsten carbide die life. PKL is formulated for acid-pickled and lime-coated rods to yield an ultra-bright metallic finish. 85 Black is fortified with fine graphite for aggressive drafting on mechanically descaled wire rods.

help_outline Can calcium-based wire drawing powder be used on mechanically descaled (MD) wire rods?

Yes. Our Grade 85 Black and Grade D-45 are engineered with balanced grain sizing and extreme-pressure additives specifically designed to bind over scale-dusted surfaces, preventing die scuffing and die choke.

help_outline Does the calcium soap film provide atmospheric rust protection on drawn wire coils?

Yes. Because calcium stearate is naturally water-insoluble and hydrophobic, the tightly adhered residual surface film acts as a protective barrier against ambient humidity during storage and inter-plant transit.
